Stage 2 — Diagnose

Finding the Real Causes.
Not the Visible Symptoms.

The Diagnose stage is where the intelligence created in Stage 1 is transformed into insight. Every data point, every stakeholder input, every mapped relationship is analysed through a single lens: what is actually causing this?

We apply root cause analysis, opportunity quantification and risk mapping simultaneously — because in complex organisations, causes and opportunities are rarely isolated. A procurement inefficiency is also a sustainability risk is also a supply chain vulnerability is also a financial exposure.

The output of Stage 2 is not a list of observations. It is a ranked, quantified register of opportunities and risks — with the evidence behind each one, the commercial case for addressing it, and the sequence in which it should be tackled.

Root Cause vs Surface Symptom

Every observed performance gap is traced back to its systemic cause — the structural, process or governance issue that created it. Surface fixes do not survive contact with reality. Structural fixes do.

Quantification Makes the Case

Every identified opportunity is quantified in financial terms — not estimated, not approximated, but calculated from the baseline data established in Stage 1. The business case exists before the recommendation is made.

Sequencing Is Everything

Not all opportunities are equal. We rank by value, feasibility and interdependence — because the right sequence of interventions compounds impact, while the wrong sequence creates interference.

What Happens in This Stage
Step 1
Root Cause Analysis
Systematic identification of the structural causes behind every observed performance gap — separating the symptom from the cause so that interventions target the right thing.
Step 2
Opportunity Quantification
Financial modelling of every identified opportunity — what it is worth, what it would cost to capture, and what the return on investment would be at realistic timescales.
Step 3
Risk Concentration Mapping
Identification and quantification of every significant risk concentration across the ecosystem — with probability assessment, potential impact and early warning indicators.
Step 4
Priority Matrix Construction
Ranking of all identified opportunities and risks by commercial impact, implementation feasibility, strategic alignment and interdependence — creating a clear, evidence-based prioritisation framework.
Step 5
Interdependency Analysis
Mapping of the relationships between identified opportunities and risks — understanding how addressing one affects another, and designing the optimal intervention sequence.
Step 6
Diagnostic Intelligence Report
A comprehensive, quantified register of opportunities and risks — with root cause evidence, commercial case, prioritisation ranking and recommended intervention sequence.
