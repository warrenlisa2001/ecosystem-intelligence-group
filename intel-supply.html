Where Organisations Lose

The Hidden Cost of
Supply Chain Blindness

These challenges do not appear on a single dashboard. They live between systems, functions and suppliers — invisible until the damage is already done.

01
You have transaction data. You don't have supply chain intelligence.
Most organisations can see what was ordered and what arrived. Very few can see the dependencies, vulnerabilities and value flows that determine whether their supply chain will perform under pressure — or fail at the worst possible moment.
EIG SolvesWe map your entire supply ecosystem — every relationship, dependency and value flow — creating the intelligence layer that transforms transactions into strategic insight.
02
Procurement is leaking value that nobody can see or quantify.
Maverick spend. Duplicate processes. Missed consolidation. Poor contract compliance. Underperforming suppliers. Each individually invisible. Together they represent 15–30% of addressable spend — quietly eroding margin every single day.
EIG SolvesOur procurement diagnostic identifies and quantifies every source of leakage across all spend categories — with a structured recovery programme to capture and protect it.
03
Resilience and efficiency are being treated as a trade-off.
The instinct after disruption is to add resilience. The instinct during cost pressure is to cut it. Both are wrong. The organisations that outperform build supply chains that are simultaneously efficient, resilient and strategically aligned — without sacrificing one for the other.
EIG SolvesWe design supply chains for total value — not just cost — balancing efficiency, resilience and sustainability to create durable competitive advantage rather than short-term savings.
04
Sustainability commitments have no supply chain foundation beneath them.
Organisations are making public sustainability commitments while their supply chains remain unmeasured, unverified and unmanaged. Regulatory pressure is increasing. Investor expectations are rising. The gap between commitment and reality is a growing liability.
EIG SolvesWe build supply chain sustainability programmes with genuine verification — assessed supplier performance, structured improvement roadmaps and credible reporting that withstands regulatory and investor scrutiny.

The Intelligent Approach

Not Cost Reduction.
Value Creation.

Traditional procurement asks: how can we spend less? Intelligent organisations ask: how can we create more value? The distinction consistently separates organisations that sustain competitive advantage from those that trade short-term savings for long-term capability.

Cost reduction alone rarely creates lasting advantage. Understanding where value is generated, protected and lost across the entire supply ecosystem does. And in today's interconnected world, supply chain performance is no longer an operational issue — it is a strategic one.

EIG helps organisations shift from managing supply chains as cost centres to managing them as ecosystems of value creation — combining intelligence, resilience, sustainability and performance into a single, coherent strategy.

Why supply chain resilience is now a board issue
+
Geopolitical uncertainty, climate-related disruption, regulatory change and resource constraints have permanently elevated supply chain risk from an operational concern to a strategic one. Boards that do not have supply chain intelligence on their agenda are flying blind on one of their most significant sources of risk and competitive advantage.
The connection between AI readiness and supply chain intelligence
+
AI is transforming supply chain management — predictive forecasting, supplier analytics, procurement automation, agentic workflows. But AI is only as effective as the intelligence foundations beneath it. Without connected data, governance and operational context, organisations simply automate their existing inefficiencies at greater speed and cost.
How sustainability and procurement intelligence converge
+
Sustainability is increasingly a supply chain issue — supplier selection, resource management, carbon exposure, regulatory compliance, long-term resilience. The organisations that lead in the future will be those that understand how sustainability, procurement and economic performance interact. This is where Sustainability Economics and Supply Chain Intelligence converge.
What a supply chain diagnostic actually reveals
+
A comprehensive supply chain diagnostic reveals far more than cost data. It maps every significant relationship, dependency and value flow. It identifies where risks are concentrating. It quantifies procurement leakage across all spend categories. It assesses supplier performance against strategic objectives. The output is a complete picture of how your supply chain is performing — and a prioritised roadmap of where the greatest opportunities for improvement exist.

How We Work

Questions We
Answer Together

Where exactly is value leaking from our supply chain?
+
We conduct a structured supply chain diagnostic — mapping every significant relationship, procurement flow and value chain stage to identify precisely where value is being created, where it is captured, and where it leaks. The output is a quantified opportunity register with a prioritised improvement programme.
How resilient are we to the next disruption?
+
We assess resilience across four dimensions: supplier concentration risk, geographic dependency, regulatory exposure and sustainability vulnerability. The output is a clear resilience profile — what would happen if your top three suppliers were disrupted simultaneously — and a structured programme to close the gaps before the disruption arrives.
How do we verify our supply chain sustainability commitments?
+
We build supply chain sustainability programmes with genuine verification — not self-reporting. This includes supplier assessments against internationally recognised frameworks, structured improvement programmes with measurable milestones, and third-party verification that satisfies investors, regulators and the board.
How do we build the foundations for AI-enabled procurement?
+
Successful AI adoption in procurement requires connected data, clear governance and operational intelligence. We build these foundations systematically — ensuring that when AI tools are deployed, they amplify genuine capability rather than accelerating existing inefficiencies.
