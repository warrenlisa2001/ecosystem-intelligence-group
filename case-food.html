Use Case Narrative

Scaling Retail F&B Efficiency

Food and beverage operators need to manage volatility in demand, supply, freshness, and margin all at once. Without connected visibility, the business absorbs the cost through stock gaps, spoilage, manual coordination, and inconsistent forecasting.

The strongest food operations do not just move inventory well — they sense demand better, waste less, and coordinate faster across the whole chain.

Challenge

Food and beverage operators need to manage volatility in demand, supply, freshness, and margin all at once. Without connected visibility, the business absorbs the cost through stock gaps, spoilage, manual coordination, and inconsistent forecasting.

  • Fragmented supply chains with limited real-time visibility and import volatility
  • Demand volatility that traditional forecasting methods fail to capture
  • Food waste and loss caused by weak stock management and inaccurate ordering
  • Operational inefficiency driven by manual processes and siloed information

What EIG assessed

  • How demand signals, ordering behaviour, and replenishment decisions interacted in practice
  • Where waste and margin erosion were being created across the supply chain
  • Which data sources could materially improve forecast accuracy and stock orchestration
  • How a more connected supply view could support both food security and commercial performance

Key Findings

Waste was a visibility problem as much as a planning problem

Teams were making ordering decisions without a strong enough operating picture of local demand and stock movement.

Forecasting needed more inputs

Historical sales alone could not explain real consumption patterns well enough.

Supply and store decisions were too disconnected

Procurement, logistics, and retail operations were not responding to the same signals.

ROI improved when orchestration improved

Better coordination across the chain created gains in both resilience and margin.

Solution Model

EIG's solution model focused on richer demand intelligence, automated inventory orchestration, and a unified supply-chain view that made daily coordination more accurate and scalable.

Predictive demand intelligence

  • Combine sales history, weather, local events, and economic signals for more granular forecasting
  • Improve ordering discipline and reduce avoidable mismatch between demand and stock

Automated inventory orchestration

  • Adjust reorder logic and distribution decisions dynamically using real-time consumption signals
  • Reduce manual intervention while improving stock availability and freshness control

Supply chain visibility suite

  • Create a centralized dashboard for shipments, supplier reliability, and cold-chain compliance
  • Give leadership a more reliable view of where risk and value leakage are emerging
