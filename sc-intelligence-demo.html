Agent 01 — Demand Signal Intelligence
Bullwhip Detection & Order Validation
Live
A purchase order has arrived from your distributor. Before procurement acts on it, Agent 01 compares it against actual point-of-sale data to detect bullwhip amplification.
⬡

Enter an incoming distributor order
and the actual point-of-sale demand.

Agent 01 detects whether this is
a genuine demand signal or a bullwhip spike.

1
Amplification ratio calculation — Agent compares the incoming order quantity against actual point-of-sale data (not the order from the tier above). Amplification ratio = order qty ÷ POS demand. Anything above 1.3 signals a bullwhip pattern.
Amplification = Order Qty ÷ Actual POS Demand
›1.3 = bullwhip flag | ›1.8 = hold for validation | ›2.5 = reject and investigate
2
Urgency vs stock position cross-check — Agent validates whether the urgency stated in the order is consistent with the actual DC stock position. An urgent order when DC has 45 days of cover is a reliability flag on the order.
IF urgency = 'critical' AND dc_cover > 21 days → MISMATCH FLAG
3
Historical amplification pattern — Agent compares this order against the last 12 orders from this distributor to identify if this is a one-off spike or a systemic amplification pattern. Systemic patterns require a structural response, not just a one-order correction.
Pattern score = Σ(amplification ratios T-12) ÷ 12
›1.2 average = systemic bullwhip — structural intervention required
4
Dampening recommendation — If a bullwhip spike is confirmed, Agent calculates the demand-adjusted order quantity — what the order should be based on actual POS data plus a calibrated safety buffer — and presents it as the recommended production target.
Dampened Qty = POS Demand × (1 + Safety Buffer %)
Default buffer: 12% | Low variance SKU: 8% | High variance: 18%
5
Financial impact calculation — Agent quantifies the cost of acting on the inflated order versus the dampened recommendation: excess inventory carrying cost, emergency freight risk if overcorrecting, and write-off exposure at end of shelf life.
Cost of bullwhip = (Order Qty − Dampened Qty) × Unit Cost × Carrying Rate
Carrying rate: 22% annually for FMCG
Agent 02 — Inventory Optimisation
Safety Stock & Reorder Intelligence
Live
Static safety stock set months ago for worst-case conditions — held at that level regardless of actual demand. Agent 02 recalculates in real time.
⬡

Select DC and product category.
Agent 02 recalculates safety stock
against actual demand variability
and your service level target.

1
Demand signal baseline (T-90) — Agent reads 90 days of actual sell-through data. Calculates mean daily demand and standard deviation per SKU per DC. This replaces the planning assumption — which is often 12–18 months old and built on forecast, not reality.
Baseline = Mean(daily demand, T-90)
σ = Standard deviation of daily demand
2
Service level factor (Z-score) — Agent applies the Z-score corresponding to the required service level. 95% = Z 1.65. 98% = Z 2.05. 99% = Z 2.33. Most organisations carry 99% safety stock for 95% service level targets — wasting 40% of their safety stock budget.
Z: 95% SL = 1.65 | 98% SL = 2.05 | 99% SL = 2.33
3
Optimal safety stock formula — The industry-standard formula applied to actual data rather than forecast data. Lead time variability is included — if your supplier's lead time varies, that variance must be included in the safety stock calculation.
SS = Z × σ × √(Lead Time days)
Where σ = demand std dev and Lead Time = supplier lead time in days
4
Reorder point calculation — Reorder point = expected demand during lead time + safety stock. Agent sets this dynamically — it adjusts every week as demand patterns shift, rather than being reviewed once a quarter in a planning meeting.
ROP = (Mean Daily Demand × Lead Time) + Safety Stock
5
Financial impact — Agent calculates the carrying cost saving from right-sizing safety stock: (Current SS − Optimal SS) × Unit Cost × 22% annual carrying rate. For a $500M FMCG, this is typically $6–10M annually.
Annual saving = (Current SS − Optimal SS) × Unit Cost × 0.22
