Use Case Narrative

FMCG Production Line Optimization

Manufacturers cannot scale reliably when downtime, quality variability, supply chain fragility, and inefficient asset use remain hidden inside disconnected systems. The operating question is simple: how do you turn plant data into better daily decisions across maintenance, production, and supply?

In manufacturing, the fastest path to better margin is often not a bigger plant — it is a smarter operating system for the plant you already have.

Challenge

Manufacturers cannot scale reliably when downtime, quality variability, supply chain fragility, and inefficient asset use remain hidden inside disconnected systems. The operating question is simple: how do you turn plant data into better daily decisions across maintenance, production, and supply?

  • Unplanned downtime from equipment failures and reactive maintenance cycles
  • Low OEE driven by process bottlenecks and underutilised assets
  • Supply chain fragility that weakens forecasting and inventory discipline
  • Quality variability that increases waste, rework, and inconsistency

What EIG assessed

  • Where downtime and yield losses were being created along the line
  • How maintenance, production, and supply data interacted in practice
  • Which process changes could be tested safely before operational rollout
  • What plant-level dashboards leaders needed for clearer daily performance control

Key Findings

Maintenance and output performance were tightly linked

Reactive maintenance was driving hidden cost across uptime, waste, and planning reliability.

OEE issues were systemic

Losses were spread across scheduling, asset health, process variation, and line coordination.

Forecasting and inventory decisions influenced factory performance

Supply planning weakness amplified plant inefficiency rather than sitting outside it.

Leaders needed simulation, not just reporting

Testing operational changes virtually reduced risk and improved confidence in execution.

Solution Model

EIG's manufacturing model combined predictive maintenance, digital-twin thinking, and supply optimisation to improve plant performance in a controlled, measurable way.

Predictive maintenance

  • Use AI models to identify likely equipment issues before failure occurs
  • Shift maintenance from reactive firefighting to planned intervention

Digital twin simulation

  • Model process changes virtually before rollout
  • Support better decisions on throughput, sequencing, and line adjustments

Supply chain optimization

  • Improve logistics and inventory forecasting so production decisions are based on stronger signals
  • Reduce the operational volatility that often shows up as plant inefficiency
